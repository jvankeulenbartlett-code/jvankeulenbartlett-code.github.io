About Me

About Me

I’m Joshua Vankeulen-Bartlett, building and documenting my path into offensive cyber security under the alias NetRunnerZero.

Before moving into cyber security, I spent 14 years in the British Army. That background shaped the way I work: disciplined, structured, calm under pressure, and focused on execution. Those same qualities now guide how I approach technical security work.

I am diploma-qualified and currently completing a BSc (Hons) in Cyber Security, while building practical offensive security skills through hands-on lab work, structured methodology, and technical experimentation.

Professional Background

My military background gave me a strong foundation in leadership, accountability, planning, and risk awareness. I am comfortable working within structure, solving problems methodically, and staying focused in demanding environments. I now bring that same mindset into cyber security, where persistence, discipline, and clear thinking matter.

Security Focus

My focus is offensive security, penetration testing methodology, and understanding how systems fail in real-world conditions. I am especially interested in enumeration, exploitation workflow, privilege escalation fundamentals, and the practical side of attack methodology.

  • Penetration testing methodology: recon, enumeration, exploitation, and post-exploitation
  • Linux and Windows environments
  • Networking fundamentals including TCP/IP, DNS, DHCP, VLANs, and troubleshooting
  • Active Directory basics and attacker-focused understanding of systems
  • Lab building, documentation, and repeatable practice

Current Lab

My current home lab is built around KVM virtualization, pfSense segmentation, Kali Linux, and vulnerable targets such as Metasploitable. I use it to practise network discovery, service enumeration, exploitation, and controlled attack workflows in a realistic but safe environment.

Development Path

Alongside formal study, I am developing practical offensive security capability through self-study, lab work, and structured experimentation. My current path includes CEH in progress, a scheduled CCNA exam, and continued project building through my portfolio.

Career Direction

I am working toward an entry-level cyber security role where I can contribute with discipline, technical curiosity, and a strong practical mindset. My long-term direction is offensive security, penetration testing, and hands-on security work.
